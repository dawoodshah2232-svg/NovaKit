The short answer: open PDFEdit's [Word to PDF](/tools/word-to-pdf) tool, drop in your `.docx` file, and download the PDF. No Microsoft Word installed, no account, no upload — the conversion runs entirely in your browser and preserves your text formatting.

## Why you'd need this

- **You're on a Chromebook, Linux machine, or someone else's computer** with no Office installed
- **Your Word trial expired** and you just need this one document converted
- **You're on your phone** and need a PDF to attach to an application or email
- **You don't want to upload a confidential document** to a random converter site

## What survives the conversion

PDFEdit's converter reads your DOCX's structure and rebuilds it as a PDF. What carries over:

- **Fonts** — matched to the closest metric-compatible open font (Calibri → Carlito, Arial → Arimo), so spacing and line breaks stay essentially the same
- **Sizes and colors** — preserved
- **Bold, italic, underline** — preserved
- **Alignment and headings** — preserved
- **Simple lists** — preserved

What's honestly skipped (the tool tells you upfront):

- **Images, charts, and text boxes** — text-only conversion
- **Headers, footers, and footnotes** — not carried over
- **Tables** — flattened; cell text is kept but the grid and borders are lost
- **Legacy .doc files** — only modern .docx is supported

For text documents — resumes, letters, essays, reports, contracts — this covers everything that matters. For image-heavy or complex layouts, use desktop Word or Google Docs' export. When in doubt, convert and check: the 30-second review described above tells you immediately whether the browser tool was sufficient.

## Step by step

1. **Check the format.** The file must be `.docx` (modern Word format). Got a legacy `.doc`? Open it free in Google Docs or LibreOffice and save/download as `.docx` first.
2. **Check the size.** The browser converter handles files up to 50 MB.
3. **Open** [Word to PDF](https://www.pdfedit.website/tools/word-to-pdf) and drop in the file.
4. **Download the PDF.** Watch the progress bar; when it's done, save the file.

## The alternatives, honestly compared

- **Google Docs:** Upload the DOCX, File → Download → PDF. Free, handles images and tables better, but uploads your document to Google.
- **LibreOffice:** Free desktop suite, excellent conversion fidelity. Requires an install.
- **PDFEdit (browser):** No install, no upload, best for text documents and sensitive files. Skips images and complex elements.

Pick based on your document: text-heavy and confidential → browser tool. Image-heavy or complex → Google Docs or LibreOffice.

## Common mistakes

**Trying to convert a .doc file.** The tool rejects it with a clear message — it's a genuinely different format. Convert to .docx first (Google Docs does this free).

**Expecting images to appear.** They won't — text-only is a documented limitation, not a bug. If your resume has a headshot, use Google Docs export instead.

**Uploading a 200 MB thesis.** The 50 MB browser limit exists because your browser's memory is doing the work. Split the document or use desktop software for monsters.

**Converting just to "have a PDF" when Word's the working format.** If you're still editing, keep the DOCX. Convert once, at the end. Every round trip between formats loses something.

## Checking the converted PDF

Don't just download and send — a 30-second check catches the known limitations:

1. **Skim every page.** Confirm nothing's missing — remember images and charts are skipped, so verify the document still makes sense without them.
2. **Check the first and last pages.** Headers, footers, and cover elements are the most likely casualties; make sure their absence doesn't break the document.
3. **Verify fonts look right.** Your Calibri became Carlito (or similar) — it should look essentially the same, but glance at headings and unusual formatting.
4. **Check tables.** Flattened tables read as sequential text. If the document's meaning depended on the grid, this conversion wasn't the right tool — use Google Docs export instead.
5. **Confirm the page count is sane.** A 10-page DOCX becoming a 40-page PDF signals a paragraph-detection quirk worth investigating before you send it.

## When the browser tool isn't enough

Honest boundaries — reach for an alternative when:

- **The document has images that matter.** A resume with a headshot, a proposal with diagrams, a report with charts — these need Google Docs (File → Download → PDF) or LibreOffice, both free.
- **Complex tables carry the meaning.** Financial statements, timetables, spec sheets — flattened tables may mislead. Use a full converter.
- **It's over 50 MB.** Thesis-length documents with embedded everything belong in desktop software.
- **You need PDF/A archival format or digital signatures.** That's Acrobat or similar territory.

For everything else — letters, essays, contracts, plain reports — the browser tool is the fastest option and keeps your file off other people's servers.
