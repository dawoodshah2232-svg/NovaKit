The short answer: open your PDF in an organize tool, drag the page thumbnails into the order you want, and export. The tool rebuilds the document with your new sequence — nothing else changes.

Page order mistakes are the most common PDF annoyance: the appendix landed before the contract, the cover page is in the middle, the merged invoices came out jumbled. Re-merging from scratch is the slow fix. Rearranging the finished file is the fast one.

## Drag-and-drop reordering

In [PDFEdit's organize tool](/tools/organize-pdf):

1. **Add your PDF.** Every page appears as a thumbnail card in a grid.
2. **Drag pages into place.** Grab any page and drop it between two others. The grid reflows live, so you see the final order as you work.
3. **Duplicate or delete while you're there.** Need two copies of a form page? Duplicate it. Spot a blank or duplicate scan? Delete it in the same session.
4. **Export.** Download the rebuilt PDF with your new page order.

It works on touch screens too — the same drag gesture works on phones and tablets.

## Jobs this fixes

**The botched merge.** You merged five files and the order came out wrong. Don't re-merge: open the merged file, drag pages into place, export. Two minutes.

**The appendix problem.** Supporting documents should go last, but they arrived first. Drag the block of pages to the end in one motion.

**The cover page.** Scans often start with a crooked cover or a separator sheet that belongs at the back (or in the bin — delete it).

**The form that needs two copies.** A form where the client signs one copy and keeps another: duplicate the page, and both copies sit in one file.

**The mixed-orientation cleanup.** While reordering, you'll spot the sideways scan. Fix it with the [rotate tool](/tools/rotate-pdf) before or after — rotation is per-page, so only the wrong one turns.

## Reordering vs. the alternatives

- **Re-merging** the source files is slower and risks introducing new order mistakes.
- **Splitting and re-combining** works but it's three operations where one drag-and-drop does the job.
- **Editing in Acrobat/Preview** is fine if you own the software, but for a quick reorder a free browser tool is faster to reach.

## Tips for big rearrangements

- **Work in passes.** For a 100-page document, first drag the big blocks into roughly the right zones (all of section B after section A), then fine-tune within zones.
- **Zoom the thumbnails** if pages look similar — invoice thumbnails all look alike at small size. Most organize tools render them large enough to read headers.
- **Delete as you go.** Rearranging is the perfect moment to drop the duplicates and blanks you spot. One export, all fixes applied.
- **Verify the export.** Scroll the downloaded file once. Page-order work deserves a 30-second sanity check before you send it anywhere. Count the pages too — the total should match what you started with (minus any you deliberately deleted, plus any you duplicated).

## Rearranging on touch devices

Drag-and-drop works on phones and tablets — the organize tool's thumbnails are touch-sized — but a few adjustments help:

- **Rotate to landscape** for more thumbnails per row; fewer scrolls to find the page you're moving.
- **Move in small batches.** Dragging one page across 80 thumbnails on a phone is fiddly. Move it roughly into place, scroll, then fine-tune.
- **Use two hands:** one finger to scroll the grid, another to hold the dragged page. (Sounds obvious; everyone tries one-handed first.)
- **For big jobs, switch to desktop.** Rearranging 100+ pages is genuinely a big-screen task. The tool is the same; the screen is the difference.

## Rearranging merged vs. scanned documents

The source affects the strategy:

**Merged documents** (assembled from separate files) usually need *block* moves — whole chapters or sections in the wrong order. Drag the block's pages as a group: move the first page of the block, then the rest follow it one by one. Tedious for 20-page blocks, but reliable.

**Scanned documents** usually need *single-page* fixes — one page out of place, a duplicate to delete, a sideways page to rotate. Here the organize tool's delete and the [rotate tool](/tools/rotate-pdf) matter as much as dragging.

**Hybrid jobs** (scan + digital originals merged) need both: block moves for the digital sections, single-page surgery for the scan. Do the scan cleanup first, then the block ordering — it's easier to position clean pages.

## When rearranging reveals bigger problems

Sometimes dragging pages around exposes that the document needs more than reordering:

- **Pages are missing, not misplaced** → you need the source file, not a page tool.
- **Content is wrong within pages** → that's editing, not organizing. A PDF editor (or the source document) is the fix.
- **The "right" order is unclear** → stop and ask the document owner before rearranging. Reordering someone else's 90-page report on a guess is how pages get lost.
- **The document needs splitting, not reordering** → if half the pages belong to a different recipient entirely, [split them out](/tools/split-pdf) instead of shuffling them to the back.
