The short version: if you like iLovePDF's toolset but want your files to stay on your device — no uploads, no account, no usage metering — browser-local tools are the alternative worth trying.

iLovePDF is popular for good reasons: it covers the standard PDF tasks well, the interface is clear, and the free tier handles casual use. This isn't a takedown. But "free tier of a server-based tool" and "free browser-local tool" are different products with different trade-offs, and it's worth understanding the difference before you upload your next contract to someone's server.

## The architectural difference (the part that matters)

**Server-based tools (iLovePDF's model):** you upload your file, their servers process it, you download the result. This is how most online PDF tools work. It's convenient and powerful — servers can throw serious compute at tasks.

**Browser-local tools (PDFEdit's model):** the processing code downloads to your browser once, and your file never leaves your device. Upload, process, download all happen on your machine.

Why this matters:

- **Privacy.** A server-based tool necessarily receives your document. Their privacy policy governs what happens to it — how long it's stored, who can access it, which jurisdiction it's in. A browser-local tool has nothing to govern: there is no copy on any server because no copy ever left your device.
- **Sensitive documents.** Contracts, medical records, financial statements, legal filings, HR documents — the files where "it's probably fine" isn't good enough. With local processing, the question doesn't arise.
- **Offline resilience.** No upload means no waiting on your connection for large files, and no failed jobs when the café WiFi drops mid-upload.
- **No accounts, no metering.** Server processing costs money per file, which is why server-based free tiers need limits and accounts. Local processing costs the provider nothing per file — which is why we don't need sign-ups, task quotas, or watermarks.

## What to honestly expect from any free tier

Every free PDF tool is free *somehow*. The models:

- **Freemium with limits** (most server-based tools): free tasks per day/hour, file size caps, or features reserved for paid plans. Fine for occasional use; annoying the moment you have a busy week.
- **Ad-supported:** free but you're the product in the attention sense.
- **Browser-local:** free because the marginal cost per file is zero. No accounts because there's nothing to meter.

None of these is a scam — they're business models. Just know which one you're using, because it predicts how the tool will treat you when you need it most.

## How to choose

Ask three questions:

1. **Where does my file go?** If the answer is "a server," read the privacy policy before uploading anything sensitive. If the answer is "nowhere — it stays in your browser," the privacy question answers itself.
2. **What happens at the limit?** Free tiers with task caps fail exactly when you're busiest. Unlimited-by-architecture tools don't have a busy-week problem.
3. **Do I need an account?** Accounts are fine when you want sync and history. For a one-off merge, they're friction.

## Switching checklist: try before you commit

Moving tools is low-risk if you test properly. Run this 10-minute evaluation:

1. **Your most common task.** Whatever you use iLovePDF for most — merging, compressing, converting — do it here with a real file. Compare the output quality.
2. **Your largest file.** Server tools sometimes handle huge files better; browser tools handle them without upload time. Test with your actual worst case.
3. **The Network-tab test.** Open DevTools → Network, run the tool, confirm nothing uploads. Thirty seconds, total certainty about privacy.
4. **A sensitive document.** Process something you'd hesitate to upload anywhere. Notice how different it feels when the file never leaves your machine.
5. **Mobile.** Try the tool on your phone. Browser-local tools work anywhere; the experience should be identical.

If all five pass, you've found your replacement. If something specific doesn't work — say you need a particular conversion quality — you've learned exactly what to keep the old tool around for. Most people find the switch is complete, and the privacy upgrade is permanent.
