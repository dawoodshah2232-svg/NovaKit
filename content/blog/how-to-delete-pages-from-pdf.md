The short answer: add your PDF to a delete-pages tool, click the thumbnails of the pages you want gone, and download the cleaned file. The original is never modified — you get a new PDF minus the deleted pages.

Deleting pages is the cleanup operation: duplicate scans, blank pages from a bad print run, the terms-and-conditions pages you don't need in your copy, the upside-down misfeed that got scanned twice.

## The visual way (recommended)

In [PDFEdit's delete tool](/tools/delete-pdf-pages):

1. **Add your PDF.** It renders every page as a thumbnail, right in your browser.
2. **Click pages to mark them for deletion.** Selected thumbnails highlight so you can see exactly what's going away. Made a mistake? Click again to unselect.
3. **Use shortcuts for patterns.** Buttons select all even or all odd pages in one click — handy for removing every other page from a duplex-scan gone wrong.
4. **Delete and download.** You get a new PDF with only the pages you kept.

Because you *see* every page before it goes, this beats typing page numbers from memory. Off-by-one errors basically disappear.

## Common deletion jobs

**Duplicate scans.** Scanners double-feed. Scroll the thumbnails, spot the twins, delete the extras.

**Blank pages.** Print runs and scans leave strays. (Dedicated workflow: [deleting blank pages](/blog/how-to-delete-blank-pages-from-pdf).)

**Unwanted sections.** Keeping a personal copy of a report without the 20-page appendix? Select the appendix range and drop it.

**Cleaning a merged file.** You merged everything, then realized two files overlapped. Delete the duplicated pages from the merged file instead of re-merging.

**Sensitive pages.** Need to share a document but not the page with internal notes? Delete it from the shared copy. (For blacking out text *within* a page rather than removing whole pages, use [redaction](/tools/redact-pdf) instead.)

## What deleting doesn't do

- **It doesn't renumber pages.** If the original had printed page numbers, deleting pages leaves gaps (pages 1, 2, 5, 6). Add fresh [page numbers](/tools/add-page-numbers) afterward if the sequence matters.
- **It doesn't shrink much.** Removing pages cuts size proportionally, but if the file is still too big to email, [compress](/tools/compress-pdf) it after deleting.
- **It's not redaction.** Deleted pages are gone from the new file — good. But if you need to remove sensitive *text* while keeping the page, that's a [redaction](/tools/redact-pdf) job, not deletion.

## Safety notes

**Your original is safe.** Delete tools create a new file; they never overwrite your upload. If you delete the wrong page, re-run the original — no harm done.

**Check the page count.** After downloading, glance at the new file's page count and scroll through once. Thirty seconds of verification beats resending a file with a missing page.

**Locked PDFs first.** A password-protected file needs [unlocking](/tools/unlock-pdf) before its pages can be edited.

## Deleting pages on your phone

The delete tool works in mobile browsers too, and the thumbnail grid is actually well suited to touch:

1. Open the [delete tool](/tools/delete-pdf-pages) in Safari or Chrome.
2. Add the PDF from your Files app or Downloads.
3. Tap thumbnails to mark pages — selected ones highlight clearly even on a small screen.
4. Delete and download; the cleaned file saves to your downloads.

It's genuinely practical for quick jobs — removing the accidental blank photo-page from a document before forwarding it. For a 200-page cleanup, though, use a desktop: the bigger thumbnails make visual scanning much faster.

## When deletion isn't the right tool

Deletion removes whole pages. For these jobs, reach for something else:

- **Hiding text on a page you're keeping** → [redaction](/tools/redact-pdf) (blacks out the sensitive bits permanently).
- **Taking pages out to keep as a new file** → [extraction](/tools/extract-pdf-pages) (same result, framed as "save these" rather than "drop those").
- **Fixing the order** → [reorganize](/tools/organize-pdf) (drag pages into place instead of deleting and re-adding).
- **Password-protecting instead of removing** → [protect](/tools/protect-pdf) (keep the pages, lock the file).

A useful rule: **delete when the pages shouldn't exist in this copy; extract when the pages deserve their own file; redact when the page stays but the content can't be seen.**

## Bulk deletion patterns

Beyond even/odd shortcuts, a few patterns cover most bulk jobs:

- **"Everything after page 10"** — select the tail range and drop it. Faster than extracting 1–10? About the same; pick whichever framing feels natural.
- **"The middle third"** — reports with a boilerplate middle section you don't need.
- **"First and last"** — cover sheets and back-page ads from downloaded PDFs.

After any bulk delete, scroll the result once. Bulk operations are where wrong-page mistakes hide.

**Deleting from merged files.** A frequent pattern: you merged a batch of files, then discovered two of the source files overlapped (the same invoice scanned twice, months apart). Rather than re-merging everything, open the merged file in the delete tool, find the duplicated run of pages via thumbnails, and drop them. The merged file is just a PDF like any other — all page tools work on it.

**Repeat deletions across similar files.** Processing twelve monthly statements that all have the same junk cover page? The delete tool works one file at a time, so it's twelve quick runs. Open the tool once and cycle through them — each run is under a minute once you're in the rhythm: add, click the cover thumbnail, delete, download, next.
