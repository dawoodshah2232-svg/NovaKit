The short answer: open a PDF compressor in Safari, add the file from your Files app, pick a compression preset, and download the smaller version. No app to install, no account, and the file never leaves your iPhone.

The usual scenario: you're away from your desk, someone needs a document emailed *now*, and the PDF is 40 MB of scanned pages that won't attach to anything. Your iPhone can fix this in about two minutes.

## Step by step in Safari

1. **Save the PDF to Files** if it isn't there already. From Mail: open the attachment, tap share, "Save to Files." From WhatsApp or Drive: share/download it into Files.
2. **Open [PDFEdit's compress tool](/tools/compress-pdf)** in Safari (Chrome on iOS works too).
3. **Tap to add the PDF.** The iOS file picker opens — browse to the file in Downloads, iCloud Drive, or On My iPhone.
4. **Choose a preset:**
   - **Recommended** — the default. Re-renders pages at 150 DPI, cuts roughly 35–65%, keeps text crisp. Right for most jobs.
   - **Extreme** — 96 DPI, cuts roughly 60–85%. For strict email limits or huge scans. Some visible softening.
   - **Less** — 220 DPI, cuts roughly 15–35%. When the file is only slightly too big and quality matters.
5. **Compress and download.** The tool shows before/after sizes. Safari saves the result to Files — tap the download icon in the toolbar to open it.
6. **Share it.** From Files, tap share and send via Mail, WhatsApp, or AirDrop.

## iPhone-specific tips

**Wi-Fi helps for big files.** Nothing uploads — but Safari does the heavy processing locally, and iPhones throttle background work more aggressively on cellular. For files over ~30 MB, Wi-Fi makes it smoother.

**Close heavy apps first.** Compression runs in browser memory. If Safari reloads the page mid-compression on an older iPhone, it's a memory squeeze — close a few apps and retry.

**Photos aren't PDFs.** If the "document" is photos in your camera roll, convert them first with an [image-to-PDF tool](/tools/image-to-pdf), then compress the resulting PDF. (Phone photos are huge — this two-step routinely turns 30 MB of photos into a 3 MB PDF.)

**Check the result before sending.** Open the compressed file in Files, zoom in on a page of text. If it's crisp, send it. If you used Extreme and the small print suffered, that's the trade-off — keep the original.

## What shrinks most on iPhone

- **Scanned documents** — the biggest wins. Phone and flatbed scans are usually far higher resolution than needed.
- **Photo collections** saved as PDF — very compressible.
- **Text-only PDFs** — barely shrink; there's no image data to cut. If one of these is "too big," it's usually embedded fonts, and compression won't help much.

## The email target

If you're compressing to email the file: Gmail and Outlook cap attachments at ~25 MB, and encoding overhead eats about a third of that in transit. Aim for **~18 MB or less** in the final file. If Extreme still doesn't get you there, [split the PDF](/tools/split-pdf) into parts instead.

## Reduce size before the PDF even exists

Often the smartest compression happens *before* you make the PDF:

**Camera settings.** If you're photographing documents, your iPhone shoots at 12–48 MP by default — absurd overkill for text. You can't easily lower the camera resolution per-shot, but scanning apps (Apple Notes scan, Adobe Scan, Google Drive) capture at sensible resolutions and produce dramatically smaller PDFs than the Camera app + image-to-PDF route.

**Scan, don't photograph.** The Notes app's document scanner (new note → camera → "Scan Documents") auto-detects page edges, corrects perspective, and outputs a compact PDF. A 10-page Notes scan is typically 1–3 MB; the same 10 pages as camera photos converted to PDF can be 30 MB.

**Choose grayscale for text.** Scanning apps usually offer color/grayscale/black-and-white. For text documents, grayscale cuts size roughly 3× versus color with zero readability loss.

## Sharing the smaller file from iPhone

Once compressed, getting it to the recipient:

- **Mail:** attach from Files (new email → attach → browse Files). Watch the ~18 MB practical ceiling for Gmail/Outlook.
- **WhatsApp/Telegram:** share directly from Files — their limits (2 GB on WhatsApp) swallow anything email can't.
- **AirDrop:** for nearby Apple devices, the fastest option and no size anxiety at all.
- **Files app link:** iCloud Drive files can be shared as links (long-press → Share → "Copy iCloud Link") for anything too big to attach.

**Pro move:** keep both versions in Files — `Contract.pdf` (original) and `Contract-small.pdf` (compressed). When someone asks for "the high-res version," you have it.

## When the iPhone struggles

Two honest limitations:

- **Very large files (100 MB+)** may exceed what mobile Safari comfortably processes. For those, use a desktop browser — the tool is identical, the RAM isn't.
- **Password-protected PDFs** need unlocking first ([unlock tool](/tools/unlock-pdf)) — the compressor can't open a file it can't read.
- **Extremely image-heavy PDFs** (photo albums, design portfolios) compress more slowly because there's simply more image data to process. Give it time; the progress indicator shows it's working.

Neither is an iPhone-specific flaw; they're physics and encryption. Everything else works exactly as on desktop.
