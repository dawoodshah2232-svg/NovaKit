The short answer: open the PDF in [PDFEdit Studio](/studio), pick the **Text** tool, click anywhere on the page, and start typing. Your text box is fully editable until export, and it becomes permanent text in the downloaded PDF.

## When a text box is the right tool

PDFs are read-only by design, but real life keeps demanding you write on them. A rental application arrives as a PDF and needs your name and date. A contract needs "agreed" next to a clause. An invoice needs a correction note. A scanned form needs filling, and the handwriting option isn't cutting it.

The text box is the answer to all of these. It drops a fresh, editable layer of text on top of the page — perfect when the original document can't (or shouldn't) be rebuilt in Word first.

## Add a text box in PDFEdit Studio

1. Open [PDFEdit Studio](/studio) and drop in your PDF.
2. Choose the **Text** tool from the toolbar.
3. Click on the page where you want the text — or drag to set the box width first.
4. Type. Use the format bar to set font, size, color, and alignment.
5. Drag the box to nudge it into position, then export.

Everything stays editable until you export: click any box to change its wording, restyle it, or move it. Undo works step by step, and the Inspector panel shows you every layer on the page so nothing gets lost.

## Match the document's look

A text box that screams "added later" undermines the document. Two quick habits fix that:

- **Sample the existing style.** Look at the font size and color of the surrounding text and match them. Body text in most business PDFs is 10–12pt dark gray or black.
- **Use a background highlight when needed.** If the original page has a light box behind its text (common on forms), give your text box the same treatment so it blends in.

Studio's format bar covers font, size, color, alignment, and background highlight — enough to make additions sit naturally on the page.

## Edit the PDF's own text instead

Sometimes a text box isn't what you want — you need to fix a typo in the document itself. Studio has a separate **Edit** tool (press **E**) for exactly that. Click a line of the PDF's own text, and Studio extracts the real text line, matches its font and size, samples its color from the page, covers the original glyphs, and hands you an editable layer styled to match. Font matching maps the original to the closest available Studio font — flagged honestly in the UI when it's an approximation rather than an exact match.

It's honest about its limits: it works on near-horizontal text in standard PDFs, matches to the closest available Studio font (flagged when it's an approximation), and tells you when a page is a scanned image with no editable text (in which case, [run OCR first](/tools/ocr-pdf)). For quick corrections — a wrong date, a misspelled name, an outdated price — it's faster than rebuilding the file.

## Text boxes vs. form fields

If the PDF already has fillable form fields, use them: click and type, and the data stays structured. Text boxes are for everything else — flat PDFs, scanned forms, and documents where the "fields" are just printed lines.

One caveat worth knowing: a text box you add is visual text, not a form field. If someone needs to tab through fields or extract data programmatically, use a real form. For humans reading and printing the document, a text box is exactly right.

## Filling forms with text boxes: alignment tricks

Flat PDF forms — the kind with printed lines instead of fillable fields — are the text box's home turf. The trick is alignment: a name that floats half a centimeter above its line looks sloppy.

- **Zoom to 150%+ before placing.** Precision at thumbnail zoom is guesswork. Zoom in, place the box, zoom out to check.
- **One box per field.** Don't stretch a single box across "Name" and "Date" — separate boxes let you align each one to its own line.
- **Match the line spacing.** If the form's lines are 24pt apart, keep your boxes on the same rhythm. Eyeball the first one, then duplicate the spacing.
- **Left-align to the line start, not the label.** The text should begin where handwriting would begin — just after the label, on the line.

For multi-page forms, place all boxes on page one first and check the look before repeating the pattern. It's faster to fix a style decision once than to reposition forty boxes.

## Common text-box mistakes

**Tiny text on a big page.** A 9pt box on a page of 12pt text looks like a footnote that escaped. Match the surrounding size.

**Boxes that cover the original text.** If you're correcting rather than adding, the [Edit tool](/studio) (E) is the right choice — it replaces the line instead of burying it.

**Forgetting to export.** Text boxes live as editable layers until you download. Closing the tab without exporting loses the work. Make "download" the last step of every session, not an afterthought.

**Using text boxes for signatures.** A typed name in a text box isn't a signature workflow — use the [sign tool](/tools/sign-pdf), which handles placement, resizing, and multi-page signing properly.
