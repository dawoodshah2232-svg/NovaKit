The short answer: open [PDFEdit's PDF to JPG tool](https://www.pdfedit.website/tools/pdf-to-jpg) in Safari on your iPhone, pick the PDF from your Files app, choose a resolution, and download. No app install, no account, no upload — the conversion happens right on your phone.

## Why not just screenshot it?

It's tempting — open the PDF, screenshot each page. But screenshots capture your screen's resolution (roughly 460 PPI on a small area, but only of what's visible), they include the PDF viewer's UI unless you crop, and doing it for a 20-page document is miserable. The converter renders each page at up to 216 DPI as a clean image with no UI chrome, handles all pages at once, and hands you a ZIP. Same effort as screenshotting one page.

## Step by step on iPhone

1. **Get the PDF into Files.** It might arrive by email, AirDrop, WhatsApp, or a download — save it to the Files app first so you can find it.
2. **Open Safari** and go to [pdfedit.website/tools/pdf-to-jpg](https://www.pdfedit.website/tools/pdf-to-jpg).
3. **Tap to upload** and choose the PDF from Files.
4. **Pick resolution:**
   - **3x Ultra (216 DPI)** — printing, or images you'll zoom into
   - **2x High-Res (144 DPI)** — the default; right for sharing, social posts, and documents
   - **1x Standard (72 DPI)** — quick previews only
5. **Download.** Save individual pages or the whole ZIP. Safari puts downloads in Files → Downloads.

### Getting images into Photos

JPGs land in the Files app, not Photos. To move one: open the JPG in Files, tap Share, tap "Save Image." For sharing directly — WhatsApp, email, social apps — you can share straight from Files without moving anything.

## Choosing JPG vs. PNG on iPhone

The sister tool, [PDF to Images](/tools/pdf-to-images), offers PNG as well. Quick rule:

- **JPG** — smaller files, great for sharing photo-heavy pages over chat apps (which compress images anyway)
- **PNG** — lossless, better for text-heavy pages where you want edges to stay crisp

If the PDF is mostly text — a form, a letter, slides — PNG at 144+ DPI will look noticeably sharper. If it's photos or you're sending via WhatsApp (which recompresses regardless), JPG is fine.

## iPhone-specific gotchas

- **Large PDFs on older iPhones.** Rendering happens in the browser, so a 200-page PDF at 216 DPI will take a while and use memory. For big documents, convert at 144 DPI, or do it on a desktop.
- **The Files app is the hub.** iOS keeps downloads in Files → Downloads. If a download "disappeared," that's where it is.
- **ZIP files open natively.** Tap a downloaded ZIP in Files and iOS extracts it into a folder — no extra app needed.
- **Private Relay / VPNs don't matter.** Since everything is local, network quirks don't affect the conversion itself — only loading the page.

- **Sending a document page via WhatsApp or iMessage** — images preview inline, PDFs don't always
- **Printing a single page** — some print workflows handle images more predictably

## Sharing directly to apps

You don't always need the JPG in Photos first. From Files:

- **WhatsApp / Telegram / iMessage:** open the JPG in Files → Share → pick the app. The image sends inline, previewed in the chat.
- **Email:** share to Mail, or attach from Files in the compose window. For multiple pages, share the ZIP — but warn the recipient, since some people don't realize iOS opens ZIPs natively.
- **Social apps:** Instagram and X accept images from the share sheet or from Photos (save there first via "Save Image").

One note: chat apps recompress images aggressively. If pixel-perfect quality matters to the recipient, send as a **document/file** rather than a photo where the app allows it (WhatsApp's "Document" option, Telegram's "Send as File") — that preserves your 216-DPI render exactly.

## Troubleshooting downloads on iPhone

- **"Where did my download go?"** Files app → Browse → Downloads. Safari always puts them there.
- **Download didn't start?** Check you have storage space, and try tapping the download link again — iOS sometimes needs a second tap on large files.
- **ZIP won't open?** It should — tap it in Files and iOS extracts it automatically. If it doesn't, the download may be incomplete; re-download.
- **Safari vs. Chrome on iOS:** both work, since all iOS browsers use the same engine underneath. Use whichever you prefer.
- **Private Browsing:** downloads still work, but the files land in the same Downloads folder — private mode doesn't hide downloaded files from the Files app.

## When JPG isn't the right output

Two cases where you should pick the PNG-capable [PDF to Images](/tools/pdf-to-images) tool instead:

- **The PDF is mostly text** — a form, letter, or slide. PNG keeps letter edges razor-sharp; JPG softens them, visibly at lower quality settings.
- **You'll edit the image afterward** — cropping, annotating, or compositing. Every JPG re-save degrades slightly; PNG doesn't.

JPG's advantage is purely file size. When size doesn't matter, PNG is the safer default.
