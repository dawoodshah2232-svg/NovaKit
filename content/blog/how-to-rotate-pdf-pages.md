The short answer: open the PDF in a rotation tool, select the pages you want — one, a few, or all — rotate them 90 degrees at a time, and download the fixed file. Unlike your PDF viewer's rotate button, this saves the change permanently.

## Why PDF pages end up sideways

Scanners are the usual culprit. The paper goes in sideways, the scanner shrugs, and your document comes out turned 90 degrees. Phone scans do it too, whenever the camera guesses the orientation wrong — which is often, especially with receipts and whiteboards. And merged PDFs mix portrait and landscape sources, so a perfectly good report can arrive with three pages doing their best impression of a sideways pancake.

It's not your fault. Sideways pages are one of the most common PDF annoyances there is, and fixing them properly takes less than a minute.

There's a subtler variant too: the PDF looks fine on your laptop but opens sideways on a colleague's phone. That's usually a viewer ignoring the rotation flag rather than a broken file — but the fix is the same. A properly rotated and saved file displays correctly everywhere, because the orientation gets written into the document instead of being left to each app's interpretation.

## How to rotate PDF pages, step by step

**1. Open a rotation tool.** PDFEdit's [rotate tool](/rotate-pdf) is free, works in your browser on any device, and needs no account. Handy when the sideways scan is sitting on your phone.

**2. Select the pages.** This is the part most people miss: you don't have to rotate everything. Select a single page, a range, or all pages. Each selection rotates independently, so the two sideways pages get fixed while the forty correct ones stay exactly as they are.

**3. Rotate 90 degrees at a time.** Click rotate left or rotate right until the page reads correctly. Most fixes need a single 90-degree turn; upside-down scans need two clicks.

**4. Check the thumbnails.** Flip through the page thumbnails before downloading. Sideways pages love hiding in long documents, and a quick visual pass catches the ones you'd otherwise miss.

**5. Download the fixed file.** Save it under a clear name, reopen it, and confirm the rotation stuck. If it opens correctly in a fresh tab, you're done.

## Rotating one page vs. the whole document

Rotate-all is for the uniform case: every page came out of the scanner sideways, so select everything and turn it once. Single-page rotation is for the mixed case: a 30-page contract where pages 14 and 15 are landscape exhibits that need turning while nothing else does.

Good tools let you combine both — fix the odd pages individually, then leave the rest alone. And if your document is a real patchwork of orientations and out-of-order pages, consider [reorganizing the pages](/organize-pdf) in the same session, so the final file reads in a sensible order from start to finish.

## The viewer trap: why your rotation didn't save

Here's the complaint we hear most: "I rotated it, but when I reopened the file it was sideways again." That's because most PDF viewers — including the one built into your browser — only rotate the temporary view. The moment you close the tab, the change evaporates. It was never written to the file.

To fix rotation for real, you need a tool that modifies the document and gives you a new file to download. That's why PDFEdit's rotate tool hands you a fresh file at the end — the download is the proof the change actually stuck. Simple rule: if your "fix" didn't involve downloading a file, it didn't stick.

## Scanned pages vs. digital pages

Rotation works the same way for both, but there's one difference worth knowing. Digital PDFs store rotation as a lightweight instruction — "display this page turned 90 degrees" — so rotating them is instant. Scanned pages are images, and some older tools physically rewrite the image data when rotating, which can take a little longer on big scans. Either way, the visual result is identical and quality is preserved. If a scanned page looks blurry after rotation, the blur was already there — rotation doesn't cause it, it just makes it easier to notice now that you can actually read the page.

## When pages are also different sizes

Mixed orientation often arrives with mixed page sizes — a US Letter report with A4 exhibits, or phone-scan receipts tucked into a longer document. Rotation fixes the angle; it doesn't normalize the size, and that's fine. Pages are allowed to be different sizes inside one PDF without any problem.

What looks odd is when a small page gets stretched to fill a big one, so if your tool offers a "fit to page size" option during rotation or merging, leave it off unless you specifically want uniform pages. Keep each page at its original size, fix the angle, and the document looks intentional instead of mangled.

## Quick tips

**Fix rotation right after scanning.** The longer a sideways PDF sits in your files, the more likely someone else opens it first.

**Rotation doesn't hurt quality.** Turning a page changes its orientation, not its content. Text and images come through exactly as sharp as before.

**Save as a new file.** Keep the original until you're sure the rotated version is correct. Disk space is cheap; rescanning a 40-page document isn't.

**Batch the job.** If you scan documents regularly, do your rotating, [merging](/merge-pdf), and page cleanup in one sitting in PDFEdit instead of fixing files one by one forever. Future you will be grateful.
